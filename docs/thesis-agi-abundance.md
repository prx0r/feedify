**GlobalFoundries** is getting more interesting the more I apply this framework.

In May 2026 it explicitly launched **Quantum Technology Solutions** around:

* cryogenic CMOS
* quantum-specific manufacturing
* advanced packaging
* materials
* scaling quantum hardware.

And crucially, it already says it has quantum customers and a pipeline. ([GlobalFoundries Inc.][2])

If AGI discovers tomorrow that:

> Architecture X can make a fault-tolerant machine using only 50,000 physical qubits.

everyone celebrates.

Then somebody asks:

> Great. Who can reliably manufacture 50,000 of the things?

That's a completely different problem.

### GFS is effectively selling reality to quantum intelligence.

It doesn't need to correctly predict:

* trapped ion
* superconducting
* spin
* photonics
* hybrid systems.

Even today's government funding involves Quantinuum working with GFS on integrated photonics and ion-trap fabrication. ([The Wall Street Journal][3])

That's exactly the thesis.

# 2. FORM may be the genuinely weird overlooked one

**FormFactor — FORM.**

This is increasingly one of my favourite quantum-adjacent research targets.

Quantum chip manufacturing has a horrible problem:

> You manufacture lots of devices.
> But which devices actually work at their operating temperature?

You can't merely test many quantum devices at room temperature.

FormFactor has automated systems that can probe **150 mm, 200 mm and 300 mm wafers at 4 Kelvin**, plus dilution refrigerators for millikelvin characterization. ([FormFactor, Inc.][4])

Their own framing is telling:

> taking superconducting device test and measurement **“out of the lab into the fab.”**

They've recently been writing specifically about the problem of testing **hundreds or thousands of cryogenic devices automatically**. ([FormFactor, Inc.][5])

That's the transition we care about.

### Quantum today

Physicist makes device → cool it → test it.

### Quantum industrialization

```text
FAB
 ↓
10,000 devices
 ↓
automated cryogenic wafer test
 ↓
yield data
 ↓
process improvement
 ↓
packaging
 ↓
quantum computer
```

AGI arguably **accelerates this bottleneck** because it can produce more designs and process hypotheses than labs can physically validate.

That is a beautiful AGI × quantum picks-and-shovels thesis.

# 3. KEYS — quantum's measurement tax

Quantum hardware is useless unless you can precisely manipulate and measure it.

Keysight sells:

* RF waveform generation
* digitizers
* microwave electronics
* synchronization
* control/readout
* benchmarking
* characterization.

Its QCS supports superconducting, spin and other qubit systems, and Keysight says it has delivered its largest-ever quantum control system already. ([Keysight United States][6])

Again:

AGI can produce immaculate control algorithms.

But that algorithm still has to produce an extremely precise physical electromagnetic pulse.

**KEYS is the bridge between intelligence and qubit reality.**

# 4. OXIG / cryogenics

Same principle.

A superconducting qubit doesn't care that GPT-9 is superintelligent.

It still wants to live around:

**10–20 millikelvin.**

That requires absurd physical machinery.

Oxford Instruments and private companies such as Bluefors sell that infrastructure.

The risk here is architecture-specificity: trapped ions and photonics don't need enormous dilution refrigerators in the same way.

So I prefer:

**FORM/GFS/KEYS > pure cryogenics**

because those businesses touch a broader set of architectures.

---

# 5. COHR/LITE — “quantum ultimately becomes an optics problem”

Potentially very interesting.

Several quantum architectures rely heavily on:

* precision lasers
* modulators
* optical components
* detectors
* photonic integrated circuits
* fiber
* interconnects.

And if modular quantum computers become the answer, connecting modules becomes essential.

The nice thing about **COHR / LITE** is that they're simultaneously exposed to the exploding **AI optical-interconnect** market.

So you get:

```text
AI scales
   ↓
optics demand

AND potentially

quantum scales
   ↓
optics demand
```

You don't need quantum to work for the business to survive.

That's structurally attractive.

# But ETH may actually be the sophisticated quantum crypto trade

This is where it gets counterintuitive.

Ethereum yesterday—**September 7, 2026**—made its position unusually explicit:

> Ethereum L1 should be quantum-resistant across execution, consensus and data by **December 2029**.

And the EF says it is deliberately planning around a possible **2030 Q-Day**, even though it acknowledges that most estimates are later. ([Ethereum Foundation Blog][15])

They're rebuilding:

* account signatures
* consensus signatures
* data commitments
* proofs

for PQ resistance.

Ethereum already has:

* a dedicated PQ team
* weekly interoperability devnets
* hash-based signatures
* leanVM
* account abstraction for signature agility
* a $1M cryptography research prize.

([ethereum.org][16])

That means an interesting possibility:

### Quantum doesn't kill ETH.

Quantum becomes another Ethereum moat.

If Ethereum successfully migrates a trillion-dollar-scale decentralized system to PQ cryptography before competitors, then:

> **credible quantum survival becomes a network property.**

The same scarcity logic applies.

Anyone can copy the algorithm.

Very few systems can migrate:

* billions in assets
* thousands of applications
* hundreds of thousands of validators
* millions of keys

without breaking everything.

### The scarce asset isn't ML-DSA.

ML-DSA is free.

### The scarce asset is a successfully migrated economic network.

That's a much deeper thesis.

# And this is why I'm less excited about generic “quantum coins”

Post-quantum cryptography itself is **not scarce**.

NIST literally publishes the standards.

Anyone can use:

* ML-KEM
* ML-DSA
* SLH-DSA.

([NIST][17])

So:

> “Our coin uses PQ crypto!”

isn't a moat.

The moat has to become:

**network + liquidity + users + applications + validators + reputation + integrations + surviving history.**

Exactly the AGI thesis again.

# And here's the Feedify alpha screen I'd actually run

We shouldn't scan “quantum news.”

We should scan for **scarcity shocks**.

For every paper / lab announcement, the agent asks:

> **If this result is true, what physical requirement increases by >10×?**

Example:

### Breakthrough

Logical qubit overhead falls 20×.

Feedify shouldn't merely return:

> bullish IONQ.

Instead:

```text
logical qubit cost ↓20×

→ viable machine dates move forward

→ quantum chip volumes ↑
→ cryogenic test volumes ↑↑
→ fab demand ↑
→ RF/control channel counts ↑
→ cryogenic cooling demand ↑
→ PQ migration urgency ↑
```

Then map:

```text
FAB                → GFS
WAFER TEST         → FORM
CONTROL            → KEYS
CRYOGENICS         → OXIG
PHOTONICS          → COHR/LITE
QPU                → IONQ/QNT/RGTI
PQ NETWORK         → ETH
PQ SPECULATION     → QRL/QANX/CELL
```

And then the **critical final query**:

> **Which asset has not moved yet?**

That's much closer to genuine alpha than “quantum is the future.”

The crypto side is especially suitable because QRL at ~$54M, QANX at ~$22M and CELL at ~$1.3M are small enough that **a genuine change in perceived Q-Day probability could produce absurd repricing**, while their tiny size also means the probability of permanent loss is correspondingly huge. The next layer I'd investigate is probably the most interesting one: **enumerate every publicly traded supplier in the physical quantum BOM—from Josephson-junction fabrication through dilution-refrigerator cabling, cryogenic amplifiers, single-photon detectors, lasers and wafer test—and find the tiny companies where quantum could move from 1% to 30% of revenue.** That's where this thesis could yield genuinely non-obvious names rather than IONQ/RGTI again.
