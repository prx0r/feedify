### The DSP Licence Moat — How Legit?

**What they claim:** "Distance Selling Pharmacy licences are no longer issued to NHS. We hold two."

**Reality:**
- DSP licences ARE real and ARE restricted. NHS stopped issuing new ones after 2013 regulations.
- **BUT** there are multiple ways to get DSP status:
  1. Acquire an existing DSP (MedPal did this — bought Universal Pharmacy)
  2. Apply through the Pharmaceutical List (possible but competitive)
  3. Convert an existing pharmacy
- **Other players with DSP licences:**
  - MedExpress (2.3M customers, bootstrapped since 2013)
  - Boots Online Doctor (major incumbent)
  - LloydsPharmacy Online
  - Chemist Direct
  - Pharmacy2U
  - Multiple smaller online pharmacies
- **The moat is real but not unique.** ~50+ DSPs exist in England. MedPal has 2 of them.

**Verdict: Moderate moat, not a fortress.**

## Red Team: What Could Kill MedPal

### 1. Lilly/Novo Go Direct
**Risk: HIGH**
- Lilly has LillyDirect (US direct-to-consumer)
- Novo has NovoCare (US direct-to-consumer)
- If they launch UK DTC, MedPal's supply advantage disappears
- Oral GLP-1s (Foundayo, Wegovy pill) could be sold directly

### 2. Price War
**Risk: HIGH**
- AIOS/Bolt is cheapest in UK (£25-42/week)
- MedPal needs to match or beat on price
- Gross margins of 34% may not survive a price war

### 3. Cash Burn
**Risk: MEDIUM-HIGH**
- £3.27M loss in H1 2026
- Needs EBITDA breakeven at 80K items/month (Oct-Nov 2026)
- If they miss, another placing needed (dilution)

### 4. AIM Listing Risk
**Risk: MEDIUM**
- Small-cap, illiquid
- 83% shares not in public hands
- Key man risk (Jason Drummond 41.8%)

### 5. NHS Reimbursement Squeeze
**Risk: MEDIUM**
- NHS prescription margins are thin
- Volume growth may not translate to profit growth

### 6. AI Claims Unverified
**Risk: MEDIUM**
- "Vertex AI triage" — no public benchmarks
- "Juno agentic AI" — no public demos
- Could be marketing language over substance

## Verdict

**MedPal is a legitimate business with real revenue and a real moat. But the moat is narrower than claimed.**

| Strength | Weakness |
|----------|----------|
| DSP licences (real but not unique) | AIOS has 15x more patients |
| Direct Lilly/Novo supply (unique) | Supply agreements can be replicated |
| Robotic dispensing (real) | Small scale (47K items/month) |
| Vertical integration (real) | Unproven AI claims |
| GLP-1 growth (real) | Cash burn until EBITDA breakeven |

**The bull case requires:**
1. Hitting EBITDA breakeven at 80K items/month
2. Oral GLP-1 driving massive volume growth
3. eMARx national roll-out succeeding
4. AI claims proving real (not just marketing)

**The bear case:**
1. AIOS crushes them on price
2. Lilly/Novo go direct
3. They miss breakeven and need another placing
4. AI claims prove hollow

**At 6.64 GBX (£51.65M market cap), the risk/reward is interesting IF they execute.** But this is a high-risk, high-reward bet on a small-cap AIM stock with unproven AI claims.

**Red team rating: 6/10.** Real business, real moat, but smaller and riskier than the pitch suggests.
