## Problems

1. **~45% is T-bills and cash** — nearly half the fund is dead weight while you pay 0.65%
2. **Mixes DRAM with NAND/storage** — different cycles, different supply/demand dynamics
3. **Missing Micron (MU)** — primary US-listed pure-play on HBM
4. **35% is already just Samsung + SK Hynix** — paying 0.65% to own two stocks
5. **Concentration**: Top 10 = 94.3% of fund

## The Thesis

The tightest node in the memory chain is **HBM yield and advanced packaging**. Whoever dominates there wins.

- **SK Hynix**: Dominant HBM supplier, 522% YTD
- **Micron (MU)**: Catching up on HBM, US-listed
- **Samsung**: Too diversified across consumer electronics, foundry, displays

## Better Play

Pick the specific HBM bottleneck winner directly:
- **SK Hynix** (000660.KR) — if you can buy Korean stocks
- **Micron (MU)** — US-listed HBM exposure
- **Samsung** — small position for HBM ramp catch-up

DRAM is a "I want memory exposure but don't want to think about it" vehicle. The fee drag + T-bill dilution + NAND noise makes it a mediocre version of what you could construct yourself with 2-3 names.
